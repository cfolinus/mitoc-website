## Why a new bunkhouse

{% include bunkhouse_summary.html part='why' %}

The existing cabin should remain a serviceable kitchen and lounge for another 10–20 years, so the bunkhouse is for sleeping only. For more, see the FAQs: [why MITOC needs the bunkhouse](/camelot/faqs#why-does-mitoc-need-the-bunkhouse), [whether the cabin can be brought up to code](/camelot/faqs#can-the-existing-cabin-be-brought-up-to-code), and [why not buy a house instead](/camelot/faqs#why-not-buy-a-house-instead).

## The plan

From 2019 through 2021, the Outing Club worked with Maclay Architects to create a bunkhouse design that complies with all modern building codes, is satisfactory to MIT Facilities and the local town board, and yet maintains the wilderness ethos of the Outing Club and its Camelot property. The new bunkhouse will be sited {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.distance_to_bunkhouse }} from the existing cabin and will provide heated sleeping space in a modern, safe, tightly insulated building enclosure. [See the full design.](/camelot/project)

## Timeline

* **2011:** Discussions begin.
* **2013:** The MITOC board approves the project.
* **2014–2018:** Focus groups.
* **2018:** A generous initial donation in memory of Joris Naiman kick-starts active development.
* **2019–2021:** Design with Maclay Architects.
* **2022–2024:** Phase 1 fundraising.
* **June 2023:** The Groton planning board approves the project.
* **2024–2025:** Phase 1 is built: a new bridge, off-street parking lot, and access trail, along with the final construction drawings.
* **2026–2027:** Phase 2 fundraising, now under way.
* **Planned for {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.groundbreaking }}:** Groundbreaking, once the full {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.total_cost }} is raised.
* **Planned for {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.completion }}:** The bunkhouse is finished.
